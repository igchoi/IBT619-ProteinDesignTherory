# De Novo Design and First-Pass Evaluation of an Alpha-Helical Antimicrobial Peptide Candidate

## Abstract

This project aimed to generate and evaluate preliminary de novo protein candidates with antimicrobial peptide-like properties. The design objective was a short, positively charged, amphipathic alpha-helical peptide that could serve as a computationally plausible membrane-interacting candidate. Five sequences were generated using simple sequence-level design assumptions, then evaluated by sequence-based metrics and ColabFold structure prediction. Among the candidates, Design B was selected as the primary final candidate because it showed the best overall balance between sequence-level amphipathic design criteria and high-confidence alpha-helical structural prediction.

## 1. Introduction

Antimicrobial peptides often contain short alpha-helical segments with amphipathic character, where cationic residues contribute to interaction with negatively charged microbial membranes and hydrophobic residues support membrane association. In this project, the design goal was not to experimentally validate antimicrobial activity, but to create a small set of computationally reasonable protein candidates and evaluate them using first-pass sequence and structure prediction criteria.

The target design was defined as a short alpha-helical peptide-like protein with moderate positive charge, controlled hydrophobicity, and minimal sequence features that might disrupt helix formation. The final output of the workflow was a prioritized candidate suitable for more rigorous structural modeling and downstream experimental or simulation-based follow-up.

## 2. Design Objective and Assumptions

The objective was to design a small antimicrobial peptide-like protein candidate with the following properties:

| Design parameter | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Protein type | Small alpha-helical functional peptide/protein |
| Functional model | Antimicrobial peptide-like membrane-interacting candidate |
| Length | Approximately 35-60 amino acids |
| Secondary structure | Single amphipathic alpha helix or short helix-turn-helix |
| Net charge | Approximately +3 to +8 |
| Hydrophobic residue fraction | Approximately 35-50% |
| Key structural feature | Amphipathic alpha-helical surface |
| Favored residues | Ala, Leu, Lys, Glu, Gln, Ser |
| Limited residues | Pro, Gly, and long hydrophobic stretches |

The residue set was chosen to support alpha-helical propensity while maintaining a balance between charged, polar, and hydrophobic residues. Lysine was used as the major positively charged residue, leucine and alanine were used to promote helix formation and hydrophobic surface formation, and glutamate, glutamine, and serine were included to tune polarity and solubility.

## 3. Candidate Generation

Five preliminary candidates were generated by varying charge distribution, hydrophobic patterning, and simple secondary structure intent.

| Design C | `EALKQLLKAQSKSGKLAEQLLKQALSKQLAEKQSQ` | Helix-turn-helix-like candidate with a short GS-containing turn |

Design B was initially intended to represent the most balanced antimicrobial peptide-like design: positively charged, amphipathic, and strongly alpha-helical without being excessively hydrophobic or excessively cationic.

## 5. Structure Prediction Workflow

The five candidate sequences were evaluated using ColabFold in a lightweight CPU-based first-pass screening mode.

```bash
/tmp/codex-install/envs/colabfold/bin/colabfold_batch \
  candidates.fasta colabfold_results \
  --msa-mode single_sequence \
  --num-models 1 \
  --num-recycle 1 \
  --overwrite-existing-results
```

This setup was used as a preliminary screen rather than a final high-accuracy AlphaFold evaluation. Because the candidates were de novo short alpha-helical sequences, `single_sequence` mode was used to avoid depending on weak or unavailable homologous MSAs.

The predicted PDB files were then collected in `predicted_structures/` and evaluated using a custom structural evaluation script. The evaluation focused on mean pLDDT, minimum pLDDT, low-confidence residue fraction, radius of gyration, and helix-like fraction.

## 8. Final Candidate Selection

The final recommended candidate was Design B:

```text
>Design_B_amp_helix_moderate
KLAKQLSEKLKQALSKQLAELKQALSKQLAELKKQS
```

Design B was selected because it provided the best compromise between the original antimicrobial peptide-like design assumptions and the structure prediction results. It showed moderate positive charge, acceptable hydrophobic fraction, the strongest hydrophobic moment among the original candidates, and a high ColabFold confidence score.

## 9. Discussion

The computational screening results support Design B as the strongest candidate for follow-up analysis. Its sequence-level features match the intended design assumptions, especially the balance of positive charge, hydrophobicity, and amphipathic character. The ColabFold prediction also supports the structural plausibility of the design by predicting a continuous alpha-helical conformation with high mean pLDDT.

However, these results should be interpreted as a preliminary computational screen. pLDDT reflects confidence in the predicted local structure, not experimental stability or antimicrobial activity. In addition, the current prediction used a lightweight ColabFold setup with single-sequence mode, one model, and one recycle. Therefore, the results are useful for prioritization but not sufficient as final validation.

## 10. Limitations

Several limitations should be considered:

- The structural prediction was performed using a lightweight screening setup rather than exhaustive AlphaFold/ColabFold sampling.
- The design was evaluated computationally only; antimicrobial activity was not experimentally tested.
- Membrane binding, selectivity, toxicity, aggregation, and protease stability were not directly evaluated.
- The pTM values are relatively low, which is expected for short single-chain peptide-like designs but should still be noted.
- Amphipathicity was estimated from sequence-level metrics rather than validated by membrane simulations or experimental assays.


## 11. Conclusion

Five de novo alpha-helical antimicrobial peptide-like candidates were generated and evaluated using sequence-level and structure-level criteria. Design B was selected as the final recommended candidate because it showed the best overall balance of amphipathic sequence design, moderate cationic character, controlled hydrophobicity, and high-confidence alpha-helical structural prediction. The results support Design B as a reasonable candidate for further computational refinement and experimental validation.
